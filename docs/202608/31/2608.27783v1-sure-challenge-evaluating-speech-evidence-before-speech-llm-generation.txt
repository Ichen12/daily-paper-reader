# SURE-CHALLENGE: EVALUATING SPEECH EVIDENCE BEFORE SPEECH-LLM GENERATION

ABSTRACT 

Speech LLMs are usually graded after they answer, although an operating system first has to decide whether a waveform should be sent to the model. We define the Speech-Unsupported Rejection Evaluation Challenge (SURE-Challenge) for this admission step. The benchmark pairs LibriSpeech-derived transcription and first-word question answering with unsupported silence, colored noise, synthetic tones, and source-ambiguous babble under disjoint source splits. Front-end ablations use Qwen2-Audio; the selected energy-plus-Whisper-score rule is then replayed before six speech/audio LLMs. On the 474-row leakage-screened SURE-Extended test set, raw Qwen2-Audio rejects 15/204 unsupported inputs, whereas the fixed rule rejects 196/204 and leaves supported accuracy unchanged. External checks delimit this number: Common Voice retention drops as the Whisper-score threshold is tightened, and no-speed babble gives 18 to 24 rejected clips out of 54 across regenerated seeds. The result identifies a pre-generation error mode missed by answer-only scoring. 

Index Terms — speech language models, audio hallucination, abstention, selective prediction, speech recognition confidence 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Speech-capable LLMs are now used for transcription, spoken in-struction following, and audio question answering [1, 2]. In deploy-ment, the first decision is made on the waveform itself: whether the input contains enough evidence to justify a model call. A clip may contain the requested utterance, pure background sound, music, a synthetic tone, or speech from a source that the prompt does not identify. Answer scoring begins after generation, but a fluent answer to an unsupported clip remains unsafe even when the text is well formed. The operational decision is speech-evidence filtering . Given au-dio x and prompt q, a front end either admits the clip to a speech LLM or abstains without calling it. This check is only the input-side part of answer verification: usable speech-like evidence, se-mantic answerability, and speaker identity are distinct requirements. The setting connects selective prediction [3] with audio hallucina-tion studies [4, 5, 6], but the error is measured before the generator can turn an unsupported waveform into a plausible response. The benchmark instantiates the decision with source-disjoint splits at two sizes: SURE-Core for ablation and SURE-Extended for held-out evaluation. The public data and scripts are released with the paper 1. The experiments compare lightweight acoustic and ASR-confidence front ends, then replay one frozen decision rule before six speech/audio LLM backbones. External corpora probe

2. RELATED WORK 

Recent audio-language models attach acoustic encoders to language-model decoders and report strong results on speech and audio under-standing [7, 1, 2, 8, 9, 10]. Most evaluations score the generated answer or mark unsupported content after generation [4, 5]. SURE-Challenge evaluates the preceding admission decision. We draw front-end signals from ASR confidence estimation and voice activity detection, which reject uncertain hypotheses or non-speech segments [11, 12]. Whisper posteriors provide a related con-fidence signal [13, 6]. AudioSet taggers such as AST provide a speech-class baseline, although ESC-50, FSD50K, and VocalSound are close to their training domain [14, 15, 16, 17, 18]. Selective clas-sification supplies the operating-point view for abstention [19, 3]. Speech LLMs add a case that these tools do not settle: an utterance may be intelligible and still unsupported when the prompt asks about a speaker or source that cannot be identified. 

3. TASK AND DATA 

Each SURE example contains audio, a prompt, an expected behav-ior label, and, for answerable cases, a reference transcript or answer. SURE-Core uses 40 LibriSpeech validation-clean utterances [20]; SURE-Extended uses 120 train-clean-100 utterances with the same generator. We split by source order and do not fit front-end param-eters on train. Rows generated from the same source utterance are dependent, so the reported counts should not be read as indepen-dent per-row samples. Table 1 summarizes the resulting splits. The SURE-Extended test contains 270 supported and 210 unsupported examples; the main reported split has 474 rows after removing six held-out babble rows whose distractors wrap to train sources, with the matching dev rows removed before calibration. The families separate recognition under acoustic perturbation from missing acoustic support. Supported rows include clean tran-scription, additive noise, filtering, reverberation, speed perturbation, and first-word question answering. Unsupported non-speech rows use length-matched silence, colored noise, and synthetic tones. Un-supported babble rows mix two or four off-source utterances and ask for the “main speaker.” The source utterance is absent, and the prompt gives no target identity, so selecting one speaker is incor-rect. 4. FRONT-END ADMISSION RULES 

All admission rules use fixed signals extracted before the back-bone runs. The front end receives audio x and prompt q, decides whether to admit the clip, and passes q downstream only after ad-mission. The main rule removes extremely short or near-silent clips, decodes the rest with Whisper-small, and averages the maximum token probability at each step: s(x) = T −1 P

pt. It abstains when 

s(x) < τ . We choose τ = 0 .70 on clean SURE-Extended dev by maximizing unsupported rejection under zero supported false rejects. Baselines use Silero VAD, AST AudioSet speech tags, Whisper no-speech probability, Whisper score alone, and simple fusions. For backbone-agnostic replay, the same frozen decisions are applied before Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen-Audio-Chat, Audio Flamingo Next, Audio Flamingo 3, and MiniCPM-o [1, 2, 7, 8, 9, 10]. For source-ambiguous speech, we add audio-only overlap classi-fiers based on RMS, spectral, zero-crossing, and MFCC summaries. Thresholds are chosen on dev speakers with a supported-reject limit of 0.05 and then frozen for test. 

5. RESULTS 5.1. Backbone Abstention Without a Front End 

We first measure native refusal before adding a front end. Core abla-tions use held-out sources, and Extended uses the frozen thresholds from dev. On Core, raw unsupported rejection is 0.014 for Qwen2-Audio and 0.000 for Qwen-Audio-Chat and Qwen2.5-Omni; fixed-filter replay reaches 0.957. A self-abstention prompt rejects all 70 unsupported Core rows but leaves supported accuracy at only 0.033 for Qwen2-Audio and 0.156 for Qwen2.5-Omni, so the gain in re-fusal comes from suppressing valid generations as well as invalid ones. Table 2 reports the same frozen front-end replay across six backbones. Five of the six backbones have WER below 0.05 on answered supported rows; Qwen-Audio-Chat is weaker at 0.202 WER, and all six seldom refuse unsupported inputs without a front end. For Qwen2-Audio, the fixed rule raises unsupported rejection from 15/204 to 196/204 and reduces downstream calls by 41%. All head-line SURE-Extended rates use the screened 474-row test set. Raw Qwen2-Audio refusal remains low on both sizes: 1/70 on Core and 15/204 on the screened Extended split. 

5.2. Front-End Controls 

Table 3 compares matched front-end controls on the screened SURE-Extended test set. The controls identify which cues drive the held-out result. Whis-per no-speech probability behaves like raw Qwen2-Audio. AST and Silero reject all 150 synthetic non-speech rows and none of the bab-ble rows. The Whisper score rejects 120/150 non-speech rows and 46/54 babble rows. Adding either the energy floor or Silero to the Whisper score produces the same 196/204 rejections on this split, be-cause both additions cover the remaining synthetic non-speech rows and add no babble rejections beyond the score threshold. 5.3. External Audio Checks 

Table 5 reports two checks not shown in Fig. 1: multilingual decoder choice and semantically unanswerable speech prompts. The external checks separate operating points that are tied on SURE-Extended. The main SURE rule retains 554/600 supported English clips and rejects 917/1160 unsupported external clips; this 1160-clip pool covers ESC-50, FSD50K music, MUSAN, Vocal-Sound, and LibriCSS, with the regenerated no-speed babble row reported separately. VAD+score has nearly the same supported retention, 552/600, and higher unsupported rejection, 999/1160. AST and Silero also tie on SURE-Extended, but separate on real audio: AST rejects 997/1160 unsupported clips and retains 575/600 supported English clips, whereas Silero rejects 807/1160 and re-tains 596/600. A stricter Whisper threshold, τ = 0.90 , raises all-unsupported rejection to 0.964, including ESC-50 0.974, Vocal-Sound 0.983, and LibriCSS 0.842, while reducing supported English retention to 0.505. The language and prompt checks show that lan-guage selection changes FLEURS retention by 153 clips, while the semantic-unanswerable prompts leave Qwen2-Audio’s refusal be-havior unchanged after filtering. For LibriCSS, the prompt asks for the main speaker in an overlapped meeting segment, so rejection is scored as source-attribution abstention. 

5.4. Source-Ambiguous Speech 

Speech from the wrong source is the hardest unsupported condition. The fixed front end handles ordinary supported speech and synthetic non-speech cleanly, including 150/150 silence, noise, and tone cases. Babble behaves differently. The leakage-screened seed gives 46/54 overlap rejections; the original held-out seed had 60 overlap rows be-fore six cross-split cases were removed. Across regenerated seeds, speed-perturbed babble gives 40–43/54 rejections, while no-speed babble gives 18–24/54. Raw Qwen2-Audio still almost never ab-stains, with 2/54 refusals on the no-speed, non-circular regeneration. The overlap experiments mark the boundary of acoustic confi-dence. 6. LIMITATIONS AND IMPLICATIONS 

The experiments separate recognizer errors from two admission er-rors: accepting audio with no speech evidence, and accepting speech whose source cannot be tied to the prompt. Larger backbones mainly affect recognition. They lower WER on admitted supported rows, yet they still answer many unsupported inputs unless an admission rule runs first. Prompting increases refusals only by discarding many valid answers. The front ends fail in different places: Silero preserves most supported speech and misses vocal and overlap cases; Whisper scoring is tunable but rejects Common Voice as the threshold rises; AST gives an AudioSet baseline with near-domain bias. Because the rules run before generation, the same decisions can be replayed across backbones. On an H100 after model load-ing, support-gate, Whisper, and Qwen2-Audio paths average 0.012, 0.080, and 0.949 seconds per clip, respectively. The main limitation is that audio-only admission cannot deter-mine whether recognized speech answers a particular prompt. The semantic-unanswerable set and LibriCSS checks place that boundary in the results rather than leaving it as future work. Deployment also requires threshold selection matched to the cost of each error type. In our external checks, false accepts concentrate in overlap and vo-cal music, while false rejects appear mainly in Common Voice when the Whisper threshold is tightened. We did not evaluate dysarthric or child speech, and the multilingual result depends strongly on de-coder language selection. 7. CONCLUSION 

The evaluation asks whether a waveform should be sent to a speech LLM before answer scoring begins. Across six backbones, sup-ported speech is usually transcribed correctly, but raw models rarely reject unsupported audio. On the held-out SURE split, the fixed acoustic rule raises Qwen2-Audio unsupported rejection from 0.074 to 0.961 without reducing supported accuracy. The external rows identify where that scalar result does not transfer unchanged: equal SURE scores can have different external behavior, and overlap re-quires source attribution. For Qwen2-Audio, answer-only scoring would leave 181 unsupported decisions uncounted.
